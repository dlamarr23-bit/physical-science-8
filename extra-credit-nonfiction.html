What the book is about

What is this book mainly about?

Try starting with: This book is mainly about... The author wants readers to understand that...

4

Retell the book

Explain the book in your own words, in order. Together, these three answers are your summary.

Beginning: How does the book start?

What topic, person, event or question does the author start with?

Try starting with: The book begins with...

Middle: What are the most important parts?

Tell two or three of the biggest ideas, events or discoveries the book explains.

Try starting with: Next, the author explains... Another important part is...

End: How does the book end?

What does the author leave you with: a result, a discovery, a warning, or what happens next?

Try starting with: At the end of the book...

5

What you learned

What are three facts you learned?

Write one complete sentence for each fact. Add the page number if you can, like (page 42).

Who is one important person in this book, and what did they do?

Pick a scientist, inventor, explorer, or someone who lived through the events. Tell what they did and why it mattered. No people in your book? Pick an important animal, place or discovery instead.

Try starting with: One important person in this book is... They... This mattered because...

Which part of our science class does this book connect to best?

Pick the one that fits best.

Explain how the book connects to science.

Use at least one science word from class, and tell how the book helped you understand it better. Picked "Another kind of science"? Name it (like biology, space or weather) and explain.

Try starting with: This book connects to science because... I learned that...
